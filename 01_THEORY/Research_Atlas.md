## Historical topology: preserve the proposal, expose the gaps

**Object type: SCHEMA.** A recovered June draft distinguishes curve, tube, overlap domain, QFP center and event. It proposes phase-shifted curves, finite-radius neighborhoods and an event localization operator. These structures are useful for organizing a visualization, but do not themselves demonstrate that spacetime or quantum correlations emerge from them.

**Object type: ANSATZ.** The historical curve family is

```text
gamma_k(theta) = (a sin(theta), b sin(theta) cos(theta),
                  c sin(n theta + phi_k))
```

**Object type: DEFINITION.** Its tube prescription is `T_epsilon(gamma) = {x in R^3 : d(x,gamma) < epsilon}`; the overlap is the intersection of selected tubes.

**Status: CONJECTURE.** The historical assertion that a sum-of-squared-distances objective always selects a unique center remains unsupported. An open overlap need not contain a minimizer; a nonconvex objective need not have a unique one. The atlas explicitly writes a candidate center as membership in an argmin set rather than silently promoting uniqueness.

**Status: CONJECTURE.** The draft's use of `integral ||gamma''(theta)|| dtheta` as “geodesic curvature” is not justified for an arbitrary parameter. A geometric treatment requires arclength and unit tangent; geodesic curvature additionally requires a specified surface or connection. The historical expression is preserved as historical, not silently repaired in its source.

**Status: CONJECTURE.** A causal order inferred from a periodic parameter requires a choice of cut or another global rule and a proof of the intended order properties. The recovered text supplies neither a complete acyclic order construction nor a physical causal mechanism.

**Object type: ANSATZ.** The inverse-distance density `sum (d+delta)^(-alpha)` is a chosen kernel. A positive regulator and the proposed zero-regulator limit must be treated separately; dimensional normalization and integrability need analysis before its integral can be interpreted as physical mass.

The draft's assertions of complete mathematical resolution or quantum-nonlocality explanation are not adopted as canonical results.

## Open questions that connect the branches

- Is the proposed continuum static, generative or a family of states? What is the mathematical object in each case?
- Is “projection” a many-to-one map, a slice, a coarse-graining, an embedding or a physical process?
- If the ground is timeless, what parameter describes folding, and what would “folding speed” mean?
- Which set or space carries ontological totality, and which subsets represent one observer's access?
- Which statements are formal correspondences, interpretive analogies or poetic resonances?
- Which additional equations and boundary data would connect a local gravitational relation to a global solution?
- What observation could distinguish any physical proposal from established alternatives?

These are research questions, not claims that the proposed connections already exist.

## Reading the graph

A `canonical-dependency` edge copies a registered scientific dependency. An `editorial-dependency` edge identifies a dependency used in the atlas's review. An `interpretive-connection`, `owner-reported-continuity` or `open-research-question` edge must not be read as a derivation, runtime connection or historical proof.
